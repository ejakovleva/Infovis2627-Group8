Task 1 – Part 2: The Audience

Dashboard concept: helping a newly licensed driver choose when to practise driving

Dashboard goal. The dashboard is designed for a young woman who has recently obtained her driving licence and wants to start practising driving independently in Barcelona. She wants to use accident data to compare different days, times and locations and choose a practice period with relatively lower recorded accident activity.

Persona

FeatureAudience profile
Domain knowledgeBasic. She has recently completed driving school, understands the basic traffic rules and how to drive, but has limited real-world driving experience.
Statistics and visualization knowledgeLow to basic. She can understand simple charts, percentages, comparisons and maps, but does not need statistical knowledge to use the dashboard.
Relevant demographic featuresYoung adult woman who has recently obtained her driving licence and is beginning to practise driving independently in Barcelona.
PlatformMobile phone. She wants to check the information quickly before deciding when to go out and practise driving.
Pain points / DesiresShe feels uncertain about driving independently and wants to choose a time when traffic-accident activity is relatively lower. She wants reassurance and simple information rather than a complex analytical interface.
What do they need to knowWhich days, hours and months have fewer recorded accidents, whether accident patterns vary by location, and how accident severity changes across time.
Frequency of useSeveral times per week, especially before planning a driving-practice session.

Scenario

A young woman has just completed driving school and obtained her driving licence. She is now ready to practise driving without an instructor, but she is still not very confident in real traffic. Before going out, she opens the dashboard on her phone and compares different days and times. She wants to find a period with relatively lower recorded accident activity and, if possible, choose an area with fewer recorded accidents. The dashboard helps her make this comparison quickly before deciding when to practise.

Interpretation: the dashboard uses recorded traffic accidents as an indicator of traffic-related risk patterns. A lower number of recorded accidents does not guarantee that a particular time or place is objectively safer for a new driver. The dashboard is intended to support comparison and awareness, not to provide a safety guarantee.

Questions the dashboard should answer

The questions are written from the perspective of the newly licensed driver and connect each user need with a visualization function and a selected chart type.

COMPARE

Which days are better for practising driving?

What the current data show: The dashboard should help compare accident activity across days of the week so that she can identify periods with relatively fewer recorded accidents.

Selected chart: Weekday bar chart
TIME

What time of day would be a better time to go out and practise?

What the current data show: Accidents are concentrated particularly in the afternoon and evening. This makes time of day an important factor to compare when choosing a practice period.

Selected chart: Hour-of-day chart / heatmap
SEASON

Are there months when there are fewer recorded accidents?

What the current data show: July has the highest number of recorded accidents (5,187), while August has the lowest (3,589). Monthly patterns can therefore help compare different practice periods.

Selected chart: Monthly bar chart
LOCATION

Which areas of Barcelona have fewer recorded accidents?

What the current data show: Eixample has the highest number of recorded accidents, with 15,080 records. A spatial view can help the user compare accident concentrations between areas.

Selected chart: Map / choropleth
SEVERITY

When are more serious accidents more common?

What the current data show: The share of severe/fatal accidents increased from approximately 2.1% in 2019 to approximately 3.3% in 2024–2025. Severity should therefore be considered alongside accident frequency.

Selected chart: 100% stacked bar chart
COMBINE

Is there a combination of day and time with relatively fewer recorded accidents?

What the current data show: Combining weekday and hour can reveal patterns that are not visible when looking at either dimension separately. This is especially relevant when choosing a specific practice session.

Selected chart: Weekday × hour heatmap
TREND

Has the overall accident situation changed in recent years?

What the current data show: Recorded accidents fell from 10,027 in 2019 to 6,268 in 2020 and then returned to roughly 7,500–8,000 per year in the following years.

Selected chart: Yearly line chart

Main findings

The current analysis suggests that time is especially relevant for this audience. Recorded accidents are concentrated particularly in the afternoon and evening, while August has the lowest monthly accident count in the dataset. The weekday-by-hour combination is therefore particularly useful because it can help the user compare specific practice periods rather than only broad categories. Spatial information is also relevant: Eixample has the highest number of recorded accidents. Finally, severity should be shown separately from accident frequency because a period with fewer accidents is not automatically a period with less severe accidents.

Dataset context: Barcelona traffic-accident records from 2019–2025. Numerical findings above are based on the EDA already performed for the project.
